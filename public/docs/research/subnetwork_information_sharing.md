# Incentivizing Information Sharing in Taskon Subnetworks

## Requirements

- **Freedom of choice**: every subnetwork must remain free to decide what information to share or keep private.
- **Incentive to share**: subnetworks that contribute knowledge should gain access to other subnetworks’ knowledge.
- **Fairness**: prevent information monopolies where large subnetworks exploit smaller ones.
- **Interoperability**: enable subnetworks to form _intersectional networks_ with selective sharing, not forced merges.
- **Transparency**: make the dynamics of sharing vs. hoarding visible to all participants.

## Possible Incentive Models

### 1. Credit-Based Sharing

- Subnetworks earn credits when they share knowledge.
- Credits are required to request or access knowledge from others.
- Prevents free-riding by subnetworks that only consume but never contribute.

### 2. Reputation-Weighted Reciprocity

- Sharing builds a reputation score.
- Higher reputation yields priority access, faster response, or broader visibility within the market.
- Reputation naturally discourages hoarding since visible hoarders lose status.

### 3. Selective Bridging

- Subnetworks can form partial overlaps with others.
- Each bridge governs what subset of knowledge flows across.
- Creates a fractal knowledge ecosystem where no single network dominates.

### 4. Transparency Incentives

- Publish clear stats: how much each subnetwork shares vs. consumes.
- Use dashboards or badges to highlight good actors.
- Social pressure motivates sharing even without direct economics.

### 5. Hybrid Market

- Combine credits (economic) + reputation (social).
- Example: credits regulate direct access, while reputation influences matchmaking and visibility.
- Encourages sustainable sharing while rewarding quality, not just volume.
